## Measured external color-context support

These primary vision studies support the narrower fact that perceived brown depends on
its light and surround context:

- `MEASURED_EXTERNAL_PRIMARY` — [Influence of achromatic surrounds on categorical
  perception of surface colors](https://doi.org/10.1016/0042-6989(89)90099-0)
  (Vision Research, 1989): isolated samples in a dark field were almost never seen as
  brown and were usually named orange; a white border less than one-sixtieth of the sample
  width restored brown perception.
- `MEASURED_EXTERNAL_PRIMARY` — [Influence of surround proximity on induction of brown
  and darkness](https://doi.org/10.1364/JOSAA.33.000A12) (JOSA A, 2016): a bright white
  surround made a yellow long-wavelength target appear browner and darker, with rapid,
  spatially localized context effects.
